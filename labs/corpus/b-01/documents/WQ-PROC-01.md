# Sampling at the tap: equipment

The equipment used for drinking water samples taken at a customer's tap, and how it is
prepared. This page is the first of two on sampling at the tap; the second covers sampling
for lead. Samples taken with the wrong bottle are rejected by the laboratory and the visit is
repeated.

## Bottles

Bacteriological samples are taken in the sterile 500 millilitre bottles with sodium
thiosulphate, sealed, from the laboratory's stock, and are not opened before the sample is
taken. Chemical samples are taken in the one litre polyethylene bottles. Metals samples,
including lead, are taken in the acid washed 250 millilitre bottles marked with a red cap.

## Labelling

Every bottle is labelled before the sample is taken with the sampling point code, the date and
time, the sampler's initials and the sample type. A bottle labelled after the sample is a
rejected sample.

## Transport

Samples travel in the cooled sample box at between 2 and 8 degrees and reach the laboratory
within six hours of the first sample of the round. The box temperature is recorded when the
box is closed and when it is opened at the laboratory.

## The sampling tap

Remove any aerator, strainer or hose. Clean the outside of the tap. Do not flame a plastic
tap. Disinfect the outlet with the laboratory's wipes and let it dry before flushing.
