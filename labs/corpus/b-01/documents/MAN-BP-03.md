# Booster pumps: pressure testing after repair

The pressure test carried out after a repair that opened a booster pump casing, replaced a
mechanical seal, or disturbed the manifold. The test protects the station against a leak that
appears at full pressure and not at the pressure the repair was checked at.

## When a test is required

After every seal replacement, every casing repair, and every manifold or non return valve
replacement. A test is not required after a filter change, a drive reset, or a coupling
alignment that did not open the hydraulic path.

## The test

Isolate the repaired pump at its suction and discharge valves. Fill and vent the casing. Raise
the pressure with the hand pump to the test pressure stated on the station's pressure test
sheet, which is set per station from the zone's maximum operating pressure, and hold it for
thirty minutes. The test passes if the pressure does not fall and no water is visible at the
seal, the casing joint or the manifold connections.

## Recording the result

Record the test pressure, the hold time and the result on the work order before it is
closed. A pump returned to duty without a recorded test is treated as untested and is taken
out of duty until one is done.

## If the test fails

Do not retighten under pressure. Depressurise, find the leak, repair, and test again from the
start. Two failed tests on the same repair are referred to the maintenance engineer.
