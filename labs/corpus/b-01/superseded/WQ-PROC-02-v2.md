# Lead in drinking water: sampling at the tap

How samples for lead are taken at a customer's tap, and how a lead service pipe is
recognised. This is version 2 of the page, in force from March 2026. It was superseded by
version 3 in June 2026, which changed the flushing step; it is kept here so that an index can
hold a stale page on purpose, and it is not counted among the twenty documents.

## Why lead samples are different

Lead in drinking water comes from the customer's own pipework or from a lead service pipe
between the main and the meter, not from the treated water. The sample has to reflect the
water that has stood in the pipe.

## Flushing before the sample

Flush the tap for five minutes at full flow, close it, and take the sample after the water
has stood for sixty minutes with no water drawn anywhere in the property.

## Taking the sample

Use the acid washed 250 millilitre bottle with the red cap. Fill it at a steady flow without
adjusting the tap, to the shoulder of the bottle. Record the stagnation time on the label.

## Lead service pipes: identification

A lead service pipe is dull grey, soft enough to mark with a key, and gives a dull sound when
tapped. It is not magnetic. The pipe is inspected where it enters the property, at the stop
tap. The finding is recorded against the connection in the customer information system.
