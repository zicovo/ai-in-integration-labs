# Gate valves: inspection and operation

How a gate valve is operated in the field, and what the technician checks before and after.
Read the maintenance schedule page for how often; this page is about how.

## Before operating

Confirm the valve's asset code against the work order. Check the network diagram for the
valves upstream and downstream, so that closing this one does not isolate a customer who was
not notified. Where a closure affects more than one property, the customer service centre
must have sent the planned interruption notice at least two working days before.

## Operating the valve

Gate valves in the Aquaflux network close clockwise. Use the valve key with the correct cap
size; a slipping key rounds the cap and the next technician cannot operate the valve at all.
Close slowly, counting turns, and stop at the first firm resistance. Do not force a valve
that stops early: a stone or a scale deposit on the seat is the usual cause and forcing it
damages the seat.

## After operating

Reopen slowly to avoid pressure surges that dislodge sediment and produce discoloured water
complaints. Record the turns to close, any resistance felt and the time of reopening.

## Appendix: when a customer refuses access

The following paragraph is copied from the collections department's disconnection procedure
for reference, because technicians ask about it. If the customer refuses access to a meter
or a stop tap during a disconnection visit, the technician does not force entry and does not
argue. The visit is recorded as refused, the work order is returned to collections, and the
collections officer decides whether to apply for a warrant. The technician's job is to
record, not to decide.
