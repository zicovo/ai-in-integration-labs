# Pressure reducing valves: maintenance schedule

The maintenance schedule for the pressure reducing valves that separate the high pressure
zones fed from the booster stations from the low pressure distribution zones. A pressure
reducing valve holds a set outlet pressure whatever the inlet does, using a diaphragm and a
pilot. It is not a gate valve and is not maintained like one.

## Every six months

Read and record the inlet and outlet pressures at the valve's gauges and compare them with
the setting on the asset sheet. Check the pilot and the tubing for leaks. Check the strainer
pressure drop; a rising drop means the strainer needs cleaning.

## Every twelve months

Clean the strainer. Check the diaphragm for pinholes by closing the isolation valves and
watching whether the outlet pressure creeps up. Exercise the isolation valves either side.

## Every five years, or as the asset sheet says

Replace the diaphragm and the pilot seals. Some valves are on a shorter interval because of
their duty; the asset sheet for the individual valve is the authority and lists what is due
at its next service.

## Records

Record every visit as a completed inspection work order against the valve's asset code, with
the pressures read. The setting itself is changed only under a separate work order, on the
setting and adjustment page.
