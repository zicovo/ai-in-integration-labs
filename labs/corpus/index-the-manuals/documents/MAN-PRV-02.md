# Pressure reducing valves: setting and adjustment

How the outlet pressure of a pressure reducing valve is adjusted, and who may authorise a
change. The setting protects the low pressure zone's pipes and the customers' plumbing; it
is not tuned by feel.

## Authorisation

A change to the set outlet pressure needs a work order from network operations naming the
new setting. A technician does not change a setting on the strength of a customer complaint
about low pressure; those go back to network operations, who check the zone model first.

## Adjusting the outlet pressure

Fit a calibrated gauge on the outlet test point. Loosen the pilot's locknut. Turn the pilot
adjusting screw clockwise to raise the outlet pressure and anticlockwise to lower it, in
quarter turns, waiting for the outlet gauge to settle between turns. When the gauge reads the
setting on the work order at a steady flow, tighten the locknut and read the gauge again.
Record the setting reached, the inlet pressure at the time and the gauge's calibration date.

## Checking the setting under flow

Open a downstream hydrant to create flow and confirm that the outlet pressure holds within
the tolerance on the work order. A valve that holds at no flow and drops under flow has a
pilot or strainer problem, not a setting problem.

## Updating the asset sheet

The new setting is written to the asset sheet by the asset register team from the completed
work order. A technician does not edit asset sheets.
